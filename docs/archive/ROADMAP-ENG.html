02 · Technical roadmap

From pact creation to payout

The protocol keeps the agreement simple, explicit, and inspectable.

Step 01

Create the project

The founder creates a project with a title/ID, description, roles, ownership shares, and a vault PDA. The project PDA is derived canonically from the creator and UTF-8 project ID.

Shipped
Step 02

Build the team

The founder adds members and roles. Members can hold multiple roles. Pending members can be removed by the founder before approval.

Shipped
Step 03

Collect approvals

Each member approves with their own signer. The founder cannot finalize until all members have approved and the share total is exactly 10,000 basis points.

Shipped
Step 04

Finalize the pact

Finalize locks the roles and shares on-chain. They cannot be rewritten after finalization; changes before finalization require the relevant approval flow again.

Shipped
Step 05

Fund the escrow

Backers fund the project vault PDA after finalization. The UI surfaces project context, open roles, remaining share, and funding state.

Shipped
Step 06

Distribute automatically

Distribution sends the protocol fee and the remaining balance to members according to their on-chain shares. The frontend fetches the project first and supplies member accounts in exact order.

Shipped
Architecture

System map

Program layer

  • Solana / Anchor 0.30 / Rust.
  • Canonical PDAs: project, vault, config.
  • Signer checks, creator ownership checks, checked arithmetic, and fee-wallet locking.
  • 13/13 LiteSVM tests reported passing, including a negative fee-redirection test.

Application layer

  • React, Vite, TypeScript, Tailwind, and Wallet Adapter.
  • Phantom, Backpack, and Solflare on desktop; Backpack is the reliable Seeker mobile option documented for demo use.
  • Supabase for signed profile access, Realtime chat, media, updates, vault Edge Function, and contact Edge Function.
  • Dedicated Helius Devnet RPC with Ankr and official RPC fallback.
Security posture

Known boundaries, stated plainly

What was fixed before the demo?
The protocol fee wallet was not originally locked on-chain in create_project; a creator could have redirected the 2% fee. A constant plus a require! check was added, deployed, and confirmed on Devnet. The program audit also verified canonical seeds, signer/ownership checks, checked arithmetic, and rent-exempt balance protection during distribution.
What is intentionally not solved in V1?
V1 funding happens after finalization, Kickstarter-style. The risk of a builder disappearing or under-delivering is visible to backers and remains a known product boundary. V2 is planned to add payment milestones, on-chain reputation, and member replacement.
Wallet demo guidance
Desktop Phantom, Backpack, and Solflare were confirmed reliable in the source status. On Seeker mobile, Backpack was confirmed reliable while Phantom and Solflare showed signature loops; the latter should not be used in a live judge demo. Durable nonce signing was abandoned because wallet anti-phishing protections block it by design.
03 · Product roadmap

Make trust useful at every stage

The product roadmap follows the shipped V1 boundary and the explicitly planned post-hackathon work.

V1 · shipped

Form the agreement

Project publishing, roles, percentage shares, multi-member approvals, on-chain finalization, and visible project context.

V1 · shipped

Discover and collaborate

Marketplace, builder directory, profiles, media, progress updates, Realtime chat, signed contact requests, and private project documents.

V1 · shipped

Fund and verify

PDA escrow, real on-chain Treasury data, distribution, Solana explorer links, and a judge-ready end-to-end path.

V2 · post-hackathon

Milestone-based payments

Move beyond post-finalization funding with staged delivery and payment milestones, reducing the risk accepted in V1.

V2 · post-hackathon

Reputation and team resilience

Add on-chain builder reputation and a defined replacement flow for members who disappear or no longer contribute.

V2 · post-hackathon

Mainnet readiness

Rotate upgrade authority to a fresh key, complete the remaining security and operational validation, then evaluate a mainnet deployment. Mainnet is deliberately out of scope for the hackathon.

Business direction

Growth without changing the promise

Start with the solo founder

One founder can frame an idea, publish needs, recruit members, lock the agreement, and coordinate funding without manually coordinating payment splits.

Earn trust through proof

Every critical claim can be checked: the pact account, approvals, escrow, and distribution are on-chain. Off-chain collaboration features add context without replacing that source of truth.

Grow the builder network

Discovery, searchable profiles, secure contact, skill levels, ratings, project updates, and shareable media create a path from an idea to a credible team.

Keep the scope honest

No unsupported user counts, TVL claims, or adoption targets are asserted here. The current source material defines capabilities and next steps, not growth metrics.
